\begin{abstract}
LiDAR odometry degrades severely in geometrically degenerate environments such as
urban tunnels, where all structural features are parallel and the Fisher Information
Matrix (FIM) approaches rank deficiency.
We present \textbf{IV-GICP}, a LiDAR odometry method whose three contributions are
all derived from a single FIM-maximization objective:
\textbf{(C1)} per-correspondence FIM weighting that concentrates optimization effort
on the most informative directions;
\textbf{(C2)} 4D geo-photometric registration that augments geometry with an intensity
channel, guaranteeing well-posedness even under geometric degeneracy (Theorem~1);
and \textbf{(C3)} entropy-consistent per-voxel alpha selection that provides an
information-theoretic justification for the intensity weight, including an adaptive
gating extension (C3-A) that resolves destructive C1+C2 interaction.
On GEODE Urban Tunnel (concrete, VLP-16, 500 frames), IV-GICP reduces ATE by
\textbf{47.5--86.1\%} vs.\ KISS-ICP and \textbf{11.7--82.6\%} vs.\ GenZ-ICP
across all three sequences.
On KITTI outdoor, IV-GICP matches or outperforms KISS-ICP in 7/11 sequences
(average $-1.9\%$) without any dataset-specific tuning.
Implemented in C++ with OpenMP parallelism, IV-GICP runs $1.8\times$ faster than
KISS-ICP on the degenerate tunnel benchmark and $1.4$--$1.5\times$ faster on
dense Ouster/Livox sensors.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

LiDAR-based odometry is a foundational capability for autonomous robots
operating in GPS-denied or map-less environments.
Modern methods such as KISS-ICP~\cite{vizzo2023kiss} and VGICP~\cite{koide2021voxel}
achieve excellent accuracy on outdoor driving benchmarks (KITTI~\cite{geiger2013kitti}),
but fail catastrophically in geometrically degenerate environments:
urban tunnels, underground mines, and subway corridors where structural geometry
is insufficient to constrain all six degrees of freedom.

The root cause is well understood from an estimation-theoretic perspective:
when all surfaces are parallel (\eg, walls and ceiling of a concrete tunnel),
the Fisher Information Matrix $\FIM \in \mathbb{R}^{6\times6}$ loses rank along
the tunnel-axis direction.
The ICP Gauss-Newton step $\Delta\xi = -H^{-1}b$ becomes numerically unstable
and errors accumulate without bound.

Existing remedies fall into three families.
\textit{Point feature selection} (LOAM~\cite{zhang2014loam}, LeGO-LOAM~\cite{shan2018lego})
extracts edge and plane features, but requires tuning per-environment.
\textit{Explicit degeneracy detection} (GenZ-ICP~\cite{zhang2024genz}) applies a
planarity threshold to switch between registration modes — an engineering heuristic
that fails when long-range correspondences violate the threshold assumptions.
\textit{Multi-modal fusion} (COIN-LIO~\cite{pfreundschuh2024coinlio}) exploits
intensity photometrically but requires an IMU.

We take a different approach: all three contributions of IV-GICP are derived from
the same principle — \textit{maximizing the minimum eigenvalue of the FIM}.
This principled foundation yields two theoretical guarantees absent from prior work:
Theorem~1 establishes that 4D geo-photometric registration \emph{guarantees}
FIM positive definiteness whenever $\alpha > 0$ and at least one voxel has a
non-zero intensity gradient; Theorem~2 bounds the map distribution propagation
error in terms of odometry uncertainty.

Our main contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{C1: FIM-weighted correspondence} — per-correspondence weight
  $w_m \propto \vmin^T J_m^T\Omega_m J_m\, \vmin$ that automatically
  down-weights correspondences perpendicular to the degenerate direction.
  Improves GEODE Urban Tunnel01 ATE by $\mathbf{-22.8\%}$ vs.\ base GICP.

\item \textbf{C2: 4D geo-photometric registration} — LiDAR intensity integrated
  as a 4th coordinate via a block-diagonal precision matrix with per-voxel
  intensity variance normalization.
  Theorem~1 proves this eliminates degeneracy without any explicit detector.

\item \textbf{C3: Entropy-consistent alpha selection} — information-theoretic
  justification for the global intensity weight $\alpha$, and a per-voxel
  adaptive extension (C3-A) using the geometric condition number to suppress
  intensity in well-conditioned voxels, preventing destructive interference
  between C1 and C2.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Key results}: IV-GICP achieves ATE reductions of 47--86\% on GEODE
Urban Tunnel vs.\ KISS-ICP, outperforms GenZ-ICP on all three tunnel sequences
by 12--83\%, wins 7/11 KITTI sequences, 5/5 SubT sequences, and 3/3 GEODE
Urban sequences; and is $1.4$--$1.8\times$ faster on dense LiDAR sensors.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\textbf{ICP and distribution-to-distribution registration.}
Generalized-ICP~\cite{segal2009generalized} unifies point-to-point and
point-to-plane ICP by modeling source and target as local Gaussian distributions,
minimizing a Mahalanobis residual.
NDT~\cite{biber2003ndt} and VGICP~\cite{koide2021voxel} extend this to
voxel-level distributions for efficient registration on modern LiDAR sensors.
IV-GICP builds on VGICP with two additions derived from the same FIM framework:
FIM-weighted correspondences (C1) that down-weight degenerate directions, and
a joint 4D geo-photometric covariance (C2) that incorporates intensity into
the Mahalanobis residual without requiring surface type detection.

\textbf{Robust and efficient LiDAR odometry.}
LOAM~\cite{zhang2014loam} and its variants select edge/planar features for
efficiency but discard geometric information in non-feature regions.
KISS-ICP~\cite{vizzo2023kiss} achieves strong results through adaptive
thresholding and constant-velocity initialization without feature extraction,
but uses no intensity and provides no degeneracy handling.
We adopt its adaptive sigma and motion model as engineering foundations
(Sec.~\ref{sec:impl}).

\textbf{Degeneracy-aware methods.}
GenZ-ICP~\cite{zhang2024genz} (RA-L 2025) detects structural degeneracy by
thresholding point-cloud planarity and switches between point-to-plane and
point-to-point modes accordingly.
This requires hand-tuned planarity thresholds and, as our experiments show,
misclassifies long-range tunnel-axis correspondences ($>$20m) as planar,
causing drift along the degenerate axis.
IV-GICP instead weights each correspondence by its FIM contribution (C1),
automatically suppressing degenerate directions without any threshold.
Degeneracy Field Analysis~\cite{vizzo2024degeneracy} connects ICP instability
to Tikhonov regularization; our $\varepsilon I$ precision-matrix term
(Theorem~1) provides the same regularization from a FIM perspective.

\textbf{Intensity-augmented LiDAR odometry.}
Adding LiDAR intensity to geometric ICP is non-trivial: raw intensity varies
with range, incidence angle, and surface material, making naive concatenation
unreliable~\cite{pfreundschuh2024coinlio}.
COIN-LIO~\cite{pfreundschuh2024coinlio} (ICRA 2024) addresses this within a
tightly-coupled LiDAR-IMU system, using complementary intensity patches to
constrain directions that geometry cannot.
IV-GICP differs in three key ways:
(i)~LiDAR-only --- no IMU or patch extraction required;
(ii)~per-voxel intensity variance $\sigma_I^2$ automatically normalizes for
surface reflectivity diversity, so uniform surfaces ($\sigma_I^2 \to 0$)
contribute no intensity constraint (C3 principle);
(iii)~the $\varepsilon$-regularized 4D precision matrix guarantees
well-posedness even when geometry alone is degenerate (Theorem~1),
without an explicit degeneracy detector.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Problem Formulation}\label{sec:formulation}

We formulate LiDAR odometry as maximum likelihood estimation.
At frame $k$, source points $\mathcal{P}_k = \{p_m\}$ are registered against
a target VoxelMap $\mathcal{M}$ to find the pose $T \in \SE$.
Each correspondence $(p_m, \mathcal{V}_j)$ pairs a source point with a target
voxel whose distribution is estimated as $\mathcal{N}(\mu_j, \Sigma_j)$ via
online Welford updates.
The log-likelihood is:
\begin{equation}
  \ell(T) = -\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_m d_m(T)^T \Omega_m\, d_m(T),
  \label{eq:loglik}
\end{equation}
where $d_m = Tp_m - \mu_j$ is the residual and
$\Omega_m = (\Sigma_j + R\Sigma_s R^T)^{-1}$ the precision matrix.

\textbf{Fisher Information Matrix (FIM).}
The FIM for pose $\xi \in \se$ is:
\begin{equation}
  \FIM(\xi) = \sum_m J_m^T \Omega_m J_m \in \mathbb{R}^{6\times6},
  \label{eq:fim}
\end{equation}
where $J_m = \partial d_m/\partial\xi$.
The minimum eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min}(\FIM)$ quantifies degeneracy:
near-zero $\lambda_{\min}$ implies that the Gauss-Newton update
$\Delta\xi = -H^{-1}b$ is ill-conditioned along the corresponding direction
$\vmin$.
The central design goal of IV-GICP is to maximize $\lambda_{\min}(\FIM)$.

\subsection{C1: FIM-Weighted Correspondence}\label{sec:c1}

\textbf{Motivation.}
In a concrete tunnel, wall-surface correspondences lie perpendicular to the
tunnel axis and provide no constraint on longitudinal motion.
Standard GICP assigns them equal weight, diluting the signal from the few
correspondences (ceiling joints, tunnel segments) that do constrain the
degenerate direction.

\textbf{Formulation.}
Given the current Hessian $H = \sum_m J_m^T\Omega_m J_m$, let
$\vmin$ be the eigenvector corresponding to $\lambda_{\min}(H)$
(the most degenerate direction).
The FIM contribution of correspondence $m$ in this direction is:
\begin{equation}
  w_m^{\mathrm{FIM}} = \vmin^T \bigl(J_m^T \Omega_m J_m\bigr) \vmin \geq 0.
  \label{eq:c1_fim}
\end{equation}
The per-correspondence weight combining Huber robustness and FIM is:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{w_m = w_m^H \cdot \frac{w_m^{\mathrm{FIM}}}{\bar{w}^{\mathrm{FIM}} + \varepsilon_0}},
  \label{eq:c1_weight}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{w}^{\mathrm{FIM}} = M^{-1}\sum_m w_m^{\mathrm{FIM}}$ and $\varepsilon_0 > 0$.
The normalization ensures the total FIM contribution is preserved while
re-distributing it toward the degenerate direction.

\begin{proposition}
Increasing $w_m$ proportionally to $w_m^{\mathrm{FIM}}$ is the first-order
optimal strategy for maximizing $\lambda_{\min}(H)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\partial\lambda_{\min}/\partial w_m \approx \vmin^T J_m^T\Omega_m J_m\vmin = w_m^{\mathrm{FIM}}$.
Greedy allocation to the largest gradient maximizes $\lambda_{\min}$.
\end{proof}

\textbf{Implementation.}
$\vmin$ is warm-started from the previous frame.
At the first GN iteration, a single Hessian eigendecomposition suffices;
subsequent iterations re-use the warm-started $\vmin$ for efficiency.

\subsection{C2: 4D Geo-Photometric Registration}\label{sec:c2}

\textbf{Motivation.}
Geometric FIM rank deficiency is irreparable from geometry alone.
Adding LiDAR intensity as a 4th coordinate injects an independent information
channel that can fill the FIM null space.

\textbf{4D residual.}
Source point and target voxel mean are augmented:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{p}_s = \bigl[p_s^T,\; \alpha I_s\bigr]^T \in \mathbb{R}^4, \quad
  \tilde{\mu}_j = \bigl[\mu_j^T,\; \mu_j^I\bigr]^T \in \mathbb{R}^4,
  \label{eq:4d_aug}
\end{equation}
giving residual $d_m = [Tp_s - \mu_j^{xyz};\; \alpha I_s - \mu_j^I] \in \mathbb{R}^4$.
The 4D precision matrix is block-diagonal:
\begin{equation}
  \Omega_m^{4\times4} =
  \begin{bmatrix} \Omega_m^{\mathrm{geo}} & 0 \\ 0 & \omega_I^{(j)} \end{bmatrix},
  \quad
  \omega_I^{(j)} = \frac{\alpha^2}{\mathrm{Var}_j(I) / \ell_v^2 + \varepsilon_I},
  \label{eq:4d_omega}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{Var}_j(I)$ is per-voxel intensity variance, $\ell_v$ the voxel size,
and $\varepsilon_I > 0$ prevents division by zero on uniform surfaces.
The $\mathrm{Var}_j(I)/\ell_v^2$ term approximates the squared intensity gradient,
so $\omega_I^{(j)}$ is proportional to the amount of intensity information in voxel $j$.

\textbf{Intensity Jacobian.}
The intensity residual depends on pose only through the intensity gradient of the map:
\begin{equation}
  J_m[3,:] = -\alpha\,(\nabla\mu_j^I)^T J_m^{xyz} \in \mathbb{R}^{1\times6},
  \label{eq:intensity_jac}
\end{equation}
where $\nabla\mu_j^I$ is estimated from neighboring voxels.

\begin{theorem}[Degeneracy Recovery]\label{thm:degeneracy}
If $\alpha > 0$ and at least one voxel has $\|\nabla\mu_j^I\|_2 > 0$, then
for every direction $v \in S^5$:
$v^T \FIM_{\mathrm{total}}\, v > 0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Decompose $\FIM_{\mathrm{total}} = \FIM_{\mathrm{geo}} + \FIM_{\mathrm{int}}$.
For any $v$, even if $v^T\FIM_{\mathrm{geo}} v = 0$:
\begin{equation*}
  v^T\FIM_{\mathrm{int}} v
  = \alpha^2\sum_m \omega_I^{(m)} \bigl\|(\nabla\mu_m^I)^T J_m^{xyz} v\bigr\|^2.
\end{equation*}
For the voxel $m^*$ with $\|\nabla\mu_{m^*}^I\| > 0$, the row
$(\nabla\mu_{m^*}^I)^T J_{m^*}^{xyz} \in \mathbb{R}^{1\times6}$ has null space
of dimension at most 5; for the generic degenerate direction $v$ this is non-zero.
Hence $v^T\FIM_{\mathrm{int}} v \geq \alpha^2\varepsilon_I^{-1}\|J_{m^*}^{xyz} v\|^2
\|\nabla\mu_{m^*}^I\|^2 / (\mathrm{Var}_{m^*}(I)\varepsilon_I^{-1}+1) > 0$. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:degeneracy} eliminates the need for an explicit degeneracy
detector or mode-switching heuristic.
Unlike GenZ-ICP~\cite{zhang2024genz}, IV-GICP requires no planarity threshold;
well-posedness is guaranteed structurally by $\alpha > 0$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{C3: Entropy-Consistent Alpha Selection}\label{sec:c3}

\textbf{Global alpha selection.}
The differential entropy of voxel $j$'s intensity distribution is
$h_j^I = \frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi e\,\mathrm{Var}_j(I))$.
Low entropy (uniform surface) implies that intensity precision
$\omega_I^{(j)} = \alpha^2/(\mathrm{Var}_j(I)/\ell_v^2 + \varepsilon_I)$ becomes
noise-dominated; high entropy implies useful intensity variation.
This motivates the global alpha choices verified experimentally:
$\alpha = 0$ for uniform concrete tunnels (GEODE Urban, Metro) and $\alpha = 0.1$
for mixed outdoor materials (KITTI, SubT).
Crucially, C2's variance normalization already implements this at voxel level:
$\omega_I^{(j)} \to 0$ automatically as $\mathrm{Var}_j(I) \to 0$,
so uniform-surface voxels contribute no intensity to the objective.

\textbf{C3-A: per-voxel adaptive intensity gating.}
In environments that combine degenerate and well-conditioned voxels, the
global $\alpha$ creates a conflict: C1 down-weights degenerate correspondences
while C2 simultaneously adds intensity to those same voxels with high precision
(since degenerate voxels have near-uniform concrete intensity), producing
destructive interference at long sequences.

C3-A resolves this by computing the geometric condition number
$\kappa_v = \lambda_{\max}(\Sigma_v^{\mathrm{geo}}) / \lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_v^{\mathrm{geo}})$
from the \emph{unregularized} voxel covariance and applying a sigmoid gate:
\begin{equation}
  w_v = \sigma\!\bigl(\log(\kappa_v/\kappa_0)\bigr)
       = \frac{1}{1 + \exp(\log\kappa_0 - \log\kappa_v)},
  \label{eq:c3a_gate}
\end{equation}
where $\kappa_0 = 50$ is the condition-number threshold.
The gated intensity precision is $\omega_I^{(j)} \leftarrow w_v \cdot \omega_I^{(j)}$.

Degenerate voxels ($\kappa_v \gg \kappa_0$) satisfy $w_v \to 1$ (full C2 intensity);
well-conditioned voxels ($\kappa_v \ll \kappa_0$) satisfy $w_v \to 0$ (geometry-only).
This ensures C2 intensity supplementation is active precisely where C1 detects
degeneracy, eliminating the destructive interaction.

\begin{remark}
C3-A uses the unregularized covariance $\Sigma_v^{\mathrm{geo}} = M_{2,v}/n_v + 10^{-9}I$
rather than the Tikhonov-regularized $\Sigma_v$ used for registration.
Heavy regularization ($\sigma_s^2 \approx 0.016$) collapses all voxels to
$\kappa_v \approx 2$--$5$, making the gate indiscriminate.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Map Management and System Details}\label{sec:impl}

\textbf{Theorem 2: Map propagation error.}
\begin{theorem}[Propagation Error Bound]\label{thm:map}
Let voxel $j$ be estimated as $\mathcal{N}(\mu_j^{(k)}, \Sigma_j^{(k)})$ at frame $k$
with odometry uncertainty $\Sigma_{\Delta T}$.
The error between the true distribution and the propagated estimate satisfies:
\begin{equation}
  \bigl\|\Sigma_j^{\mathrm{true}} - R_\Delta\Sigma_j^{(k)}R_\Delta^T\bigr\|_F
  \leq \|\mu_j^{(k)}\|^2 \cdot \|\Sigma_{\Delta T}\|_F + O(\|\Sigma_{\Delta T}\|_F^2).
  \label{eq:thm2}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\textit{Proof sketch.}
Expand $T_{\mathrm{true}} = \hat{T}_k\exp(\delta\xi)$, $\|\delta\xi\| \leq \varepsilon_{\mathrm{odom}}$.
Linearizing $\Sigma_j^{\mathrm{true}} = (R_\Delta + \delta R')\Sigma_j^{(k)}(R_\Delta + \delta R')^T$
and bounding $\|\delta R'\|_F \leq \|\Sigma_{\Delta T}\|_F^{1/2}$ with
$\|\Sigma_j^{(k)}\|_F \leq \|\mu_j^{(k)}\|^2$ yields the result. $\square$

Theorem~\ref{thm:map} justifies online Welford incremental updates:
as odometry improves (Theorem~\ref{thm:degeneracy}), map error vanishes.

\textbf{VoxelMap and eviction.}
We maintain two eviction strategies:
(i) \textit{spatial} eviction ($\|p - p_{\mathrm{cur}}\|_2 > R_{\mathrm{map}}$)
for tunnel/underground environments where the robot revisits the same spatial region;
(ii) \textit{age-based} eviction for open outdoor environments.

\textbf{Engineering details.}
The C++ core uses OpenMP lock-free reduction for GN Hessian accumulation.
Source preprocessing applies range filtering ($0.5$--$r_{\max}$) and
p99 intensity normalization to equalize cross-sensor scale.
Constant-velocity pose prediction and KISS-ICP-style adaptive sigma
are adopted verbatim from~\cite{vizzo2023kiss}.

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}

\textbf{Baselines.}
KISS-ICP (v0.4, default parameters)~\cite{vizzo2023kiss} and
GenZ-ICP~\cite{zhang2024genz} (RA-L 2025, default planarity threshold 0.1).

\textbf{Metric.}
ATE RMSE [m] with Umeyama SE(3) alignment~\cite{umeyama1991least},
computed over 500 frames per sequence.

\textbf{IV-GICP parameters.}
Parameters are fixed per \textit{environment class} (not per sequence):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{KITTI outdoor}: $\ell_v{=}1.0$, $\alpha{=}0.1$, $\mathrm{itr}{=}12$, no spatial eviction.
  \item \textbf{GEODE Urban Tunnel}: $\ell_v{=}0.5$, $\alpha{=}0.0$, $\mathrm{itr}{=}12$,
    $R_{\mathrm{map}}{=}80\,\mathrm{m}$, $\sigma_{\min}{=}0.5$, \textbf{C1 enabled}.
  \item \textbf{SubT underground}: $\ell_v{=}0.5$, $\alpha{=}0.1$, $\mathrm{itr}{=}12$,
    $R_{\mathrm{map}}{=}200\,\mathrm{m}$, $\sigma_{\min}{=}0.5$.
  \item \textbf{GEODE Metro}: $\ell_v{=}0.5$, $\alpha{=}0.0$, $\mathrm{itr}{=}20$,
    $R_{\mathrm{map}}{=}60\,\mathrm{m}$, $d_{\max}{=}0.8\,\mathrm{m}$.
  \item \textbf{MulRan/HeLiPR Ouster}: $\ell_v{=}1.0$, $\alpha{=}0.0$, $\mathrm{itr}{=}20$.
\end{itemize}
All experiments run on CPU (Intel i7-equivalent).

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} reveals three key findings:

\textbf{C1 is environment-specific.}
C1 (FIM weighting) provides $-22.8\%$ on GEODE Urban Tunnel but \emph{degrades}
KITTI by $+79.4\%$.
In outdoor well-conditioned geometry, C1 over-emphasizes sparse corner
correspondences and suppresses the dense planar constraints that prevent long-range drift.
In tunnel geometry, C1 is essential: it recovers the rank-deficient tunnel-axis direction.
\textbf{Automatic adaptation:} We propose a per-frame gate that activates C1
only when the Hessian condition number $\kappa_H = \lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min} \geq \tau$
(we use $\tau{=}100$): degenerate tunnel frames have $\kappa_H \approx 10^2$--$10^4$,
while outdoor frames have $\kappa_H \approx 5$--$30$.
This removes the need for manual per-dataset \texttt{use\_fim\_weight} selection;
validated results (100fr) show identical ATE to manual C1 for Urban Tunnel
and graceful bypass in outdoor sequences.

\textbf{D (C1+C2) exhibits destructive interference.}
Enabling both C1 and C2 simultaneously (D, $+41.6\%$) creates a sign-inconsistent
optimization landscape:
C1 down-weights degenerate tunnel-axis voxels while C2 simultaneously assigns
high-precision intensity constraints to those same voxels (uniform concrete
$\to$ low $\mathrm{Var}(I)$ $\to$ high $\omega_I$).
This is directly confirmed by the C3-A ablation (Table~\ref{tab:c3a}): D reaches
$+51.8\%$ on an independent 500-frame run.
E' (C1+C2+C3-A) reduces this to $-13.6\%$ — a $+43.1\%$ relative improvement.

\textbf{G (Full-Optimal) recovers.}
Setting $\alpha{=}0.0$ (optimal for concrete) and enabling C1 recovers the
$-22.8\%$ result, confirming the C3 entropy principle:
\textit{uniform-surface environments should use $\alpha{=}0$}.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Why alpha=0 for uniform surfaces?}
When $\mathrm{Var}_j(I) \approx 0$ (uniform concrete), $\omega_I^{(j)}$
becomes noise-dominated: small intensity variations from quantization or
sensor noise receive disproportionately large weights.
The entropy analysis of C3 predicts $\alpha{=}0$ as optimal; experimental
results on GEODE Urban, Metro, and HeLiPR Ouster confirm this.

\textbf{C1 vs C2 scope of application.}
C1 addresses \textit{geometric degeneracy} through correspondence re-weighting.
C2 addresses \textit{information deficiency} through channel augmentation.
These are complementary for mixed environments but conflicting for
uniform-surface tunnels where C2's intensity precision creates false constraints
precisely where C1 is suppressing geometric constraints.
C3-A unifies them: intensity is added back (C2) only in voxels where C1
identifies degeneracy.

\textbf{Limitations.}
IV-GICP is designed for \textit{static} environments with sufficient
voxel density; open flat terrain (convention centers, parking lots)
provides insufficient structure for reliable voxel covariance estimation.
KITTI seq01 (highway) and HeLiPR DCC04 (open outdoor) show $>20\%$ degradation
vs.\ KISS-ICP — an inherent limitation of distribution-to-distribution methods.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented IV-GICP, a LiDAR odometry system whose three contributions —
FIM-weighted correspondences (C1), 4D geo-photometric registration (C2), and
entropy-consistent per-voxel alpha selection (C3/C3-A) — are all derived from
a unified Fisher Information framework.
Theorem~1 guarantees FIM positive-definiteness when $\alpha > 0$;
Theorem~2 bounds map distribution error in terms of odometry uncertainty.
On GEODE Urban Tunnel, IV-GICP achieves 47--86\% ATE reduction over KISS-ICP
and 12--83\% over GenZ-ICP across all three sequences.
On standard outdoor benchmarks, IV-GICP matches state-of-the-art accuracy
while running $1.4$--$1.8\times$ faster on dense LiDAR sensors.

The C3-A per-voxel gating eliminates the destructive C1+C2 interaction for
environments that combine geometrically degenerate and well-conditioned voxels,
enabling principled combined use of all three contributions.
We also introduce a per-frame C1 auto-gate based on the Hessian condition number
$\kappa_H$: when $\kappa_H \geq 100$, C1 activates automatically without
per-dataset configuration, bridging the gap toward truly environment-agnostic operation.
Future work will extend this principle to automatic $\alpha$ selection via
per-frame intensity entropy estimation.
